Consensus causal graph

Adjacency matrix

Each cell is one relationship: its color gives the direction, its shade the strength, and the letter its shape. The default set is the consensus graph — relationships found in all three fits. Hover a cell for details; select any name to see its neighborhood.

Edge inventory

Retained edges

Every relationship the model retained, one per row: source, target, direction, shape, and causal score — the median across the three fits (hover for its range). Sign strength, from 0 to 1, says how consistently the effect runs in one direction. "Fits" is how many of the three fits kept the edge; those kept in all three form the consensus graph.

Function-valued causal influence

Effect curves

How does one variable act on another across its range? For each edge in the consensus graph, this view moves the source variable from its lowest observed values to its highest and traces how the receiver's prediction responds — revealing whether the relationship is steady, has a threshold, or levels off. The curve is drawn three times, for country-years in the bottom, middle and top third of the clean elections index. Both axes are in standardized units.

Forecasts

Forecast from the last observed year

For any country and variable: the observed series, and the forecast made from the last year of data for one, three, five and ten years ahead. The shaded bands show where the model expected the middle half and 90% of outcomes to fall; in testing the wider band caught the outcome 80 to 87% of the time, so treat it as somewhat too narrow. The ten-year point has not been checked against outcomes. The table below gives the chance of a large fall or rise at each horizon.

What if

How other forecasts change

What would next year look like if one variable were different today? Pick a country and a variable, and the chart shows how next year's predicted value changes for every variable it has an edge into, if it stood half a standard deviation higher or lower. The responses are read from the same fitted relationships the matrix and the effect curves show, at this country's own position on each curve. These are one-year, direct effects, so they are small: a half standard deviation shift in one variable typically moves another by about one hundredth of a standard deviation the following year.
